Embroidery and monogram patterns for wool felt hats

Stitch your hat.

Two alphabets, eight sauna motifs and a leaf wreath, all at true size. Trace, stitch, and your hat is easy to find on the peg.

Thick felt is kind to beginnersSauna hat felt is about 4–5 mm thick and does not fray. Stitches sit on top and hold their shape.

Keep the inside smoothThe inside of the hat touches your head. Where you can, take shallow stitches that stay in the outer part of the felt, and keep knots small.

Go slow, pull gentlyPull each stitch just snug: tight stitches pucker felt. Push the needle with a thimble rather than forcing it.

What you need

  • Thread:six-strand cotton floss (use 2 or 3 strands), pearl cotton or fine crewel wool.
  • Needle:a sharp embroidery or chenille needle with an eye big enough for your thread.
  • Thimbleand small pliers or a rubber grip, to pull the needle through thick felt.
  • Transfer:tracing paper or tissue paper, tear-away stabilizer, or a chalk pencil. See page 6.
  • Small scissorsand a few pins or clips.

Eight stitches

All you need for the patterns in this pack.

Running stitchUp, down, leave a gap. For dotted lines and steam.
Back stitchEach stitch goes back to meet the last one. Crisp lines and letters.
Stem stitchSmall slanted stitches that overlap like a rope. Smooth curves.
Split stitchCome up through the middle of the last stitch. Good outlines for satin.
Satin stitchClose side-by-side stitches that fill a small shape. Keep shapes under 1/2 in wide.
French knotWrap the thread twice around the needle, go down next to where you came up.
Lazy daisyA small loop held down by one tiny stitch. Leaves and petals.
Whip stitchSlanted stitches over an edge. For sewing on a felt patch.

Where to stitch

Drawings are not to scale. Hold your design against your own hat before you start.

Spacing a ring of motifs

DIVELUX hats measure about 24 in around. That fits 12 motifs 2 in apart, or 8 motifs 3 in apart. Cut out the strip, lay it along the hat and mark each spot with chalk or a pin.

Transfer and finish

Your hat stays dry. Start with a water-free method.

Water-free · recommended

Tissue or tracing paper

  1. Trace the design onto tissue paper or thin tracing paper with a pencil.
  2. Pin or baste it in place on the hat.
  3. Stitch right through the paper and the felt.
  4. Tear the paper away gently, holding the stitches with a thumb. Pick out small bits with tweezers.
Water-free

Tear-away stabilizer

  1. Print or trace the design onto tear-away embroidery stabilizer. Some brands go through a home printer: check the package.
  2. Pin, baste or stick it on, stitch through it.
  3. Tear it off along the stitches, as the package describes.
Water-free

Template and chalk

  1. Cut the motif out of the page, or glue it to thin card first.
  2. Hold it on the hat and draw around it with a chalk pencil (dark felt) or an air-erasable fabric pen (light felt).
  3. Stitch over the line. Brush leftover chalk off with a dry brush.
Uses a little water

Wash-away stabilizer

  1. Stitch through it like the tear-away kind.
  2. Tear and trim away as much as you can, dry.
  3. Most wash-away stabilizers need water to dissolve. Follow the stabilizer maker’s directions, but only dab the leftover bits with a cotton swab dipped in cool, room-temperature water (about 68–75 °F / 20–24 °C).
  4. Never hold the hat under the tap or put it in a bowl. Press with a dry towel, squeeze, don’t wring, and let it air-dry flat.

Finishing and care

Weave thread tails under a few stitches on the inside instead of tying big knots. Skip beads, sequins, metal charms and metallic thread: metal gets hot in the sauna, and beads can snag. Natural-fiber threads (cotton, wool) suit wool felt.

Wear the hat dry, as always. If it ever needs a wash, hand-wash only in cool, room-temperature water (about 68–75 °F / 20–24 °C, never warm or hot), squeeze, don’t wring, and air-dry. No washing machine, no dryer.

Pick a contrastLight thread on dark felt, dark thread on light felt. Hold the skein against the hat in daylight first.

One or two colorsA single color looks calm and modern. Add a second only for small accents like knots or leaves.

Thread lengthWork with about 18 in (45 cm) of thread at a time. Longer pieces tangle and wear thin.
